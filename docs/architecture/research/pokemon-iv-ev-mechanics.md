## TL;DR — the five facts the port branch must reckon with

| Mechanic | Range / shape | Set when | Mutable? | One-line role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Base stat** | integer, species-wide | species (fixed) | no (except evolution) | the dominant term; ceiling-ish |
| **IV** | **0–31**, one per stat | at generation | **no** (Hyper Training only *overrides the effect*) | innate per-stat potential |
| **EV** | **0–252 per stat**, **510 total** | zero at birth, **earned** | **yes — up AND down** | trained growth |
| **Nature** | one of **25**; ±10% to two non-HP stats | at generation | **no** (Mints only *override the effect*) | fixed disposition |
| **Level** | **1–100** | earned via XP → recalculates stats | yes (monotonic up) | whole-stat growth engine |

Two **corrections** to the ticket's draft framing fall out of this (detailed in [§6](#6-corrections--gotchas-the-ticket-got-slightly-wrong)):

1. **The draft formula doubles the IV.** It wrote `((Base + IV) × 2 + …)`. The real formula is `(2×Base + IV + ⌊EV/4⌋)…` — the `×2` applies to **Base only**; **IV is added once**. Confirmed by the worked Garchomp example.
2. **EVs can decrease.** The ticket asked "whether they can ever decrease" — **yes**. EV-reducing Berries (−10), Reset Bag, Fresh-Start Mochi, and Lady Clear all remove EVs.

## 1. Individual Values (IVs)

> "A Pokémon's IVs are fixed (and persisted within its data structure) **when it is generated by the game** (i.e. when it is encountered in the wild or given to the player by an NPC), and **cannot be changed** (although Hyper Training causes stats to become values corresponding to the maximum IVs)." — [Individual values](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Individual_values)

- **Range:** `0–31` per stat (since Gen III; was `0–15` in Gen I/II). ["In Generation III, the IV system was completely overhauled; now, IVs range from 0-31 rather than 0-15."](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Individual_values)
- **Granularity:** one IV per stat — **HP, Attack, Defense, Sp.Atk, Sp.Def, Speed** (six). Gen I/II stored only four (Attack, Defense, Speed, Special), deriving HP from the others.
- **Assignment:** "A Pokémon's IVs are **randomly assigned and uniformly distributed**, but with guaranteed minimum values for certain types of encounters." (e.g. raid/Titan/legendary guarantees of some perfect-31 IVs.) Each value is an independent uniform draw on `{0..31}` unless a floor applies.
- **Mutability — effectively none.** The stored IV never changes. **Hyper Training** (Gen VII+) is *not* an IV change: it makes the stat *behave as if* the IV were 31, but the underlying IV is unchanged (still used for Hidden Power, breeding). So: **IV is fixed at birth, per-stat, and never truly moves.** This is the cleanest "innate baseline" in the system.

**Read across to map #178:** this is the canonical instance of the IV concept decision [#179](https://github.com/wulke/premier-league-baseball/issues/179) already locked — *"IV = fixed innate baseline (never moves; not a ceiling/cap)."* Pokémon's IV is exactly that.

## 2. Effort Values (EVs)

> "Effort values are what cause a trained Pokémon to have higher stats than an untrained counterpart of the same level. For every 4 EVs gained, a level 100 Pokémon will have 1 extra point in its stats. Variance of stats caused by EVs reaches a maximum of 63 points at level 100, as a Pokémon can gain a maximum of 252 EVs in a single stat." — [Statistic → Effort values](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Statistic)

### Accrual — how EVs are earned

- **Primary route: defeating Pokémon in battle.** The defeated species grants 1, 2, or 3 EVs to a *specific* stat (since Gen III). ["Defeated Pokémon give out 1, 2 or 3 effort points to a particular stat, depending on species."](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Effort_values) (Gen I/II instead granted EV equal to the *base stats* of the defeated species, with a 65535 per-stat ceiling — a different, rougher system.)
- **Accelerators:** the held **Macho Brace** doubles battle EVs; **Pokérus** (a rare virus) doubles battle EVs; they stack (×4). **Power Items** (Gen IV+) add a flat +4 per defeated foe (+8 from Gen VII). **Exp. Share** spreads the foe's base yield to non-participants (but *not* the Macho Brace / Power Item bonus of the holder). **SOS Battles** (Gen VII) double the yield of called allies.
- **Stat-recalculation timing:** Gen III/IV recalculate stats on **level-up** (or on Vitamin/Berry use / PC box transfer). **Gen V+ recalculates after every battle**, and level-100 Pokémon can finally gain EVs from battles (they couldn't in Gen III–IV).

### Caps

- **Per-stat cap: 252** (since Gen VI; was 255 in Gen III–V). The usable maximum is 252 because the formula does `⌊EV/4⌋` — anything above 252 is wasted, so Gen VI hard-capped at 252 to free the points for elsewhere. ["individual stats now max out at 252 EVs instead of 255"](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Effort_values)
- **Total cap: 510** across all six stats. Since only `508` are ever effective (510 − the 2 wasted to rounding), the classic "perfect" spread is **252 / 252 / 4**.
- **Effect at level 100:** every 4 EV = +1 stat point; 252 EV → +63 stat points vs an untrained peer (before Nature).

### Items that add EVs

- **Vitamins** (HP Up, Protein, Iron, Calcium, Zinc, Carbos): **+10 EV** each to one stat. Prior to Gen VIII they couldn't push a stat past 100 EV; they can never push the total past 510. From Gen VIII the per-stat-100 limit was lifted (they work up to the 252 cap).
- **Feathers / Wings** (Gen V+): **+1 EV** each, and crucially **ignore the old vitamin per-stat-100 limit** — they can be consumed all the way to the 252/510 caps.

### Can EVs decrease? — **Yes.** (Resolves the ticket's open question.)

This is the fact the ticket explicitly flagged as uncertain. EVs are **fully reversible**:

- **EV-reducing Berries** (Gen III+, the "stat-lowering" Berries: Pomeg=HP, Kelpsy=Atk, Qualot=Def, Hondew=Sp.Atk, Grepa=Sp.Def, Tamato=Speed): **−10 EV** per use (and +friendship). (Gen III/IV quirk: if the stat was above 110, one Berry dropped it straight to 100; from Gen V it's a clean −10 every time.)
- **Reset Bag** (Gen VI Super Training), **Fresh-Start Mochi** (Scarlet/Violet's Teal Mask), **Perilous Soup** (Gen VI juice), and the NPC **Lady Clear** (Sw/Sh Isle of Armor; also Legends: Z-A): **reset all EVs to zero.**
- The **Effort Ribbon** is awarded at the 510 cap and is *retained* even if EVs are later lowered — a cosmetic footprint, not a lock.

So EV is **bounded `[0, 252]` per stat / `[0, 510]` total, earnable by battle+items, and reducible on demand.** It is **non-negative** — there is no "−EV" in Pokémon.

**Read across to map #178:** decision [#179](https://github.com/wulke/premier-league-baseball/issues/179) deliberately **generalizes this to `[−C, +C]`** (symmetric, signed EV) so slumps/aging can be the *same* quantity flowing negative — a *reimagined* departure, not a port. Pokémon's one-sided, reversible-but-non-negative EV is the thing the port branch must replicate faithfully and the reimagined branch must deliberately diverge from.

## 3. Nature

> "A Pokémon's Nature usually affects the value of two of its stats, ultimately **increasing one of its non-HP stats** … **by 10% and decreasing another by 10%**." — [Nature → Mechanics](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nature)

- **Count: 25 Natures.** ["Every Nature represents one of the 25 unique possible combinations of stat increase and decrease."](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nature) It's the 5×5 matrix over the five non-HP stats (Attack, Defense, Sp.Atk, Sp.Def, Speed): pick one to boost, one to hinder.
- **The trade:** a Nature picks **one stat ×1.1 and a different stat ×0.9**. **HP is never touched.** A stat may equal its pre-Nature value only if it's neither the boosted nor the hindered one.
- **Five are neutral:** Bashful, Docile, Hardy, Quirky, Serious — these "increase and decrease the same stat," cancelling out (net ±0 everywhere). So 20 Natures actually move stats, 5 are cosmetic.
- **Effect is a post-hoc multiplier.** Nature is applied *after* the base formula, as the final `× Nature` factor (1.1 / 0.9 / 1.0) — see [§5](#5-the-canonical-combination--the-formula). This is why it reads as "±10% of the final stat," not ±10% of Base.
- **Fixed at generation.** ["A Pokémon's Nature is determined when it is generated by the game … A Pokémon's nature cannot be changed once generated."](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nature) (Generation method: Gen III/IV derived it from the personality value; Gen V+ stores it independently.)
- **Mutability — none, like IVs.** **Mints** (Gen VIII+) are the Nature analogue of Hyper Training: a Mint *overrides the stat effect* (the "stat alignment") to that of another Nature, but **does not change the actual Nature** — flavor preference, Toxel's evolution form, Everstone breeding, and Synchronize wild-Nature inheritance all still key off the original Nature. So as a *mechanic*, Nature is immutable; the Mint is a display/stats override.
- **Side effects (out of scope for the pattern, noted for completeness):** Nature also sets flavor like/dislike (for Pokéblocks/Poffins) and, in a few spinoffs, purification efficacy / animation speed. The stat effect is the only part the port branch cares about.

**Read across to ticket [#183](https://github.com/wulke/premier-league-baseball/issues/183) (does a Nature/Disposition earn a place, or fold into IV?):** Pokémon's Nature is the canonical "fixed disposition that trades two stats" — a *separate, post-hoc, non-uniform* modifier distinct from the uniform-per-stat IV. It is the reference implementation #183 must justify keeping, simplifying, or dropping on the reimagined side.

## 4. Level

> "When a Pokémon grows a level, its stats will increase. For each level gained (ignoring Nature), stats will increase by 1/50 the base stat value, and 1/100 the combined individual value and effort value." — [Statistic → Level](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Statistic)

- **Range: 1–100.** 100 is the cap in every mainline generation. (Pokémon: Magikarp Jump goes beyond, but that's a spinoff.)
- **It scales the whole stat — Level is the growth-over-time engine.** In the canonical formula the entire `(Base + IV + EV)` term is multiplied by `Level/100` (see [§5](#5-the-canonical-combination--the-formula)); HP even adds a second `+ Level`. So leveling raises *every* stat simultaneously and proportionally, with IV/EV contributions scaling right alongside Base.
- **It is earned, monotonic, and irreversible in practice.** XP from battles fills an experience bar; crossing a threshold raises Level by 1; stats then recompute (and recompute again at the end of every battle from Gen V+ as EVs trickle in). A Pokémon never loses levels. (The Gen VI *Reset Bag* lowers EVs, not Level; there is no in-game level-lowering short of evolving into a lower-Base form.)
- **Per-level marginal gain:** the quote above gives the exact marginal — `+Base/50 + (IV+EV)/100` per level (pre-Nature) — which is just the formula differenced by one level.

**Read across to map #178:** decision [#179](https://github.com/wulke/premier-league-baseball/issues/179) explicitly **removes Level** from the reimagined model ("aging = −EV drift … Pokémon-style Level as a separate multiplier … carries both, double-counts effort — the Level-trap"). So the port branch carries Level faithfully; the reimagined branch deliberately does not, and this section documents exactly what the port is keeping and the reimagined is shedding.

## 5. The canonical combination — the formula

From [Statistic → Formula → Generation III onward](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Statistic). Floor brackets `⌊ ⌋` denote truncation (round-toward-zero).

```
HP        = ⌊ ( 2×Base + IV + ⌊EV/4⌋ ) × Level / 100 ⌋ + Level + 10

OtherStat = ⌊ ( ⌊ ( 2×Base + IV + ⌊EV/4⌋ ) × Level / 100 ⌋ + 5 ) × Nature ⌋
```

where `Nature ∈ {0.9, 1.0, 1.1}` (hindering / neutral / boosting).

**Anatomy of the terms — what each knob does:**

| Term | Knob | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `2×Base` | Base (species, fixed) | dominant; doubled so it dominates IV/EV at a given Level |
| `+ IV` | IV (0–31, fixed at birth) | innate per-stat offset — added **once**, NOT doubled |
| `+ ⌊EV/4⌋` | EV (0–252 per stat, 510 total, earned) | trained offset; the `/4` floor is why caps are 252/510 not 255/510 |
| `× Level / 100` | Level (1–100, earned) | scales the whole `(Base+IV+EV)` block — the growth engine |
| `+ 5` (non-HP) / `+ Level + 10` (HP) | constant floor | minimum stat (non-HP min 4-ish; HP min 11) |
| `× Nature` (non-HP only) | Nature (0.9/1.0/1.1, fixed) | post-hoc ±10% on exactly two non-HP stats |

**Worked example — Level 78 Garchomp, Adamant (Atk↑/Sp.Atk↓),** straight from Bulbapedia, verifying the arithmetic and the "IV added once" rule:

```
HP    : ⌊(2×108 + 24 + ⌊74/4⌋) × 78/100⌋ + 78 + 10
       = ⌊(216 + 24 + 18) × 78/100⌋ + 88
       = ⌊258 × 78/100⌋ + 88 = ⌊201.24⌋ + 88 = 201 + 88 = 289

Attack: ⌊( ⌊(2×130 + 12 + ⌊190/4⌋) × 78/100⌋ + 5 ) × 1.1⌋
       = ⌊( ⌊(260 + 12 + 47) × 78/100⌋ + 5 ) × 1.1⌋
       = ⌊( ⌊319 × 78/100⌋ + 5 ) × 1.1⌋
       = ⌊( 248 + 5 ) × 1.1⌋ = ⌊278.3⌋ = 278          (note: 2×Base=260, IV=12 added once, EV=47)

Sp.Atk: ⌊( ⌊(2×80 + 16 + ⌊48/4⌋) × 78/100⌋ + 5 ) × 0.9⌋ = 135
Speed : ⌊( ⌊(2×102 + 5 + ⌊23/4⌋) × 78/100⌋ + 5 ) × 1.0⌋ = 171   (neutral Nature → ×1.0)
```

Observe in the Attack line: `2×Base = 260`, then `+ 12` (the IV, added **once**), then `+ 47` (`⌊190/4⌋`). This is the line that contradicts the ticket's draft `((Base + IV) × 2 …)`, which would have doubled the 12 to 24.

> The lowest a stat can ever be (Shedinja's HP aside) is **4** (non-HP) / **11** (HP), per the formula's constant floors.

## 6. Corrections & gotchas the ticket got slightly wrong

1. **The draft formula double-counts the IV.** The ticket's example — `Stat = floor(((Base + IV) × 2 + floor(EV/4)) × Level/100) + 5`, then Nature — puts IV inside the `× 2`. The primary source is unambiguous: the multiplier is `(2×Base + IV + ⌊EV/4⌋)…`; **only Base is doubled; IV is a single additive offset.** Confirmed by the Garchomp Attack computation above (`260 + 12 + 47`, not `260 + 24 + 47`). The port branch must use the single-additive form.
2. **EVs can decrease — the ticket left this open, and the answer is yes.** EV-reducing Berries (−10), Reset Bag, Fresh-Start Mochi, Perilous Soup, and Lady Clear all remove EVs. (See [§2](#2-effort-values-evs).) This matters for #184's "rust vs aging" semantics: Pokémon already has a *reversible* EV, just one-sided (non-negative).
3. **"Never change" needs a footnote for IV and Nature.** Both are immutable *as stored values*, but each has an in-game **effect-override** item (Hyper Training for IV, Mints for Nature) that makes the stat behave as if the value were different without altering the underlying value. For a faithful port this is a cosmetic distinction (the stat output is what matters); for the reimagined side it's a hint that "innate baseline" can be *effectively* re-tuned without breaking the immutability invariant.
